The Roadster’s battery is made of many tiny tubes called **2170 cells**.  
They are the same size as the cells in a Model 3. [source needed]  
Thousands of cells are stacked and glued into the car’s frame, so the battery also makes the car’s body stronger. [source needed]  

The pack can store about **200 kWh** of electricity.  
That is enough to run a house for a day. [source needed]  
It also lets the car go from 0 to 60 mph in less than two seconds. [source needed]  

When you plug the car in, the battery sends a steady flow of **direct current (DC)** – like a one‑way river of electricity.  

The car has **two electric motors**, one at the front and one at the rear.  
Each motor is a **permanent‑magnet synchronous motor**.  
Imagine a small fridge magnet on a spinning wheel inside a coil of copper wire.  
The magnets pull and push the wheel, making it spin fast.  

Having two motors lets the car send power to each wheel separately, like a skateboard that can steer itself.  

Next to each motor is a **silicon‑carbide inverter**.  
It changes the battery’s DC into **three‑phase alternating current (AC)** – a kind of electricity that changes direction – which the motor needs.  
It also captures energy when you brake. This is called **regenerative braking**.  
It works a bit like a bike that recharges its battery when you slow down. [source needed]  

A **liquid‑coolant loop** runs through the battery, motors, and inverters.  
It is like a garden hose that carries a cool liquid to keep everything from getting too hot or too cold.  
This protects the cells and helps the car stay fast.  

All these parts work together to give the Roadster its speed.
