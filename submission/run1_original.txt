│  The Tesla Roadster’s power comes from a giant lithium‑ion battery and a high‑speed electric motor. The battery is built    │
│  from 2170‑type cylindrical cells – each about the size of a pen – packed into twelve modules that together hold roughly    │
│  200 kWh of energy. A liquid‑cooling loop runs through the pack, keeping the cells at a safe temperature while a Battery    │
│  Management System watches every cell’s voltage and temperature, balancing them and preventing over‑charge or deep          │
│  discharge. Because the cells are low‑impedance and arranged at a high voltage (around 400 V), the pack can deliver a lot   │
│  of power without drawing huge currents, which reduces heat loss and helps the car achieve an EPA‑rated 620‑mile range.     │
│                                                                                                                             │
│  The Roadster’s motor is a three‑phase permanent‑magnet synchronous motor that spins up to about 20,000 rpm. Permanent      │
│  magnets on the rotor create a steady magnetic field; the stator windings are switched on and off by a silicon‑carbide      │
│  (SiC) inverter, producing a rotating magnetic field that pulls the rotor around and creates torque. SiC devices switch     │
│  faster and waste less energy than older silicon parts, allowing the motor to produce up to 1,900 horsepower while staying  │
│  about 95 % efficient at cruising speeds.                                                                                   │
│                                                                                                                             │
│  When the driver lifts off the accelerator, the motor reverses its role and acts as a generator. The same SiC inverter      │
│  feeds the recovered electricity back into the battery, a process called regenerative braking. The BMS controls how         │
│  quickly the battery can accept this energy (up to 300 kW) to keep cell temperatures safe, extending range and reducing     │
│  wear on the brakes.                                                                                                        │
